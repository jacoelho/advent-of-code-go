The source is [jacoelho/advent-of-code-clj at 6018040](https://github.com/jacoelho/advent-of-code-clj/tree/601804012ca87c06ff004daeb94bc3fd3064bf03). Independent calculations establish all 49 personal answers. The [oracle records](2021-oracles.json) retain exact input hashes, methods and proof records. The [Python calculator](oracles/2021.py) uses only the standard library and repository fixtures:

```sh
python3 migration/oracles/2021.py --output /tmp/2021-oracles.json
```

Each day owns its parser, state and puzzle rules. Existing numeric conversion, scanner, grid, collection and heap helpers are reused. Day 13 uses the shared six-row OCR decoder; its official five-row example is verified as folded pixels rather than treated as a six-row font.

## Source corrections and shared input boundaries

Depth comparisons retain only the previous window. Submarine navigation checks arithmetic in the selected navigation mode. Binary diagnostics derive complementary gamma/epsilon bits and retain valid ratings when columns or rows are identical. Bingo marks every matching cell, records only actual winners and resolves simultaneous wins by input order.

Vent lines use exact numeric parsing and overflow-safe endpoint distances. Each rasterized line and the total sparse coverage are limited to one million points; a full signed-range span fails before iteration.

Triangular crab fuel checks the integer candidates around the mean, including unoccupied positions. Heightmap risk handles singleton grids; basin components follow the puzzle's non-nine connectivity assumption, independently verified on the personal input. Completion scoring excludes already-complete lines. Octopus flashes belong to completed steps, and repeated nonsynchronizing grids return an error. Polymer pairs without insertion rules persist.

The private `digit_grid.go` owns rectangular digit validation for heights, octopus energy and risk maps. Both parsed and expanded risk grids are limited to one million cells. Risk search derives tile values lazily, excludes starting-cell risk and uses the existing heap with an explicit destination.

Paper points and folds use exact shared numeric parsing with coordinates from zero through one million. Reflection may produce negative positions; rendering preserves them and bounds the display area. The personal six-row raster independently contains 89 lit dots and decodes to `GJZGLUPJ`.

Cave paths are counted on finite states containing the current cave, visited small caves and whether the optional second visit has been used. Reverse reachability first removes states that cannot lead to the end. Reverse topological counting then either computes the exact finite count or detects a useful cycle that creates infinitely many complete routes. Dead large-cave cycles do not invalidate finite answers. Limits bound small-cave bits, states and transitions; path counts use checked unsigned arithmetic.

## Exact searches and program reduction

Day 16 evaluates packets through a bounded bit cursor. Declared payload lengths fence child reads; comparison operators require exactly two operands, and remaining bits must be zero padding. Both parts validate the complete transmission, including checked value arithmetic.

Day 17 supports targets wholly below the launch point, including targets to the left or across the vertical axis. Vertical velocities range from the target's minimum y through its negation minus one; horizontal velocities include zero and both signs as needed. Targets touching or above y=0 return an unsupported-geometry error. Candidate and trajectory-step limits prevent excessive enumeration.

Day 18 stores ordered regular leaves with their depths. Explosion precedes splitting, including eligible pairs below depth four. Each addition owns its leaves, and part two distinguishes input indices even when their values are equal. The independent oracle uses immutable nested trees.

Day 19 generates the twenty-four signed coordinate permutations with positive determinant. Unique input beacons and translation counts establish twelve distinct overlaps. Each successful scanner contributes to the global beacon set; a pass without progress returns an alignment error. Input limits bound scanner count, beacon count and coordinate arithmetic; separate limits bound translation candidates and comparisons.

Day 20 stores finite exceptions to an explicit infinite background. Each step derives the next background from lookup index zero or 511, and updates only the exception rectangle plus its one-cell border. Empty exception sets are valid. A lit final background produces an infinite-answer error.

Day 21 reads the two starting positions and owns its die and memo state per solve. The quantum search uses a fixed array indexed by both positions and scores; scores increase on each transition. Weighted universe totals use checked unsigned arithmetic.

Day 22 keeps a disjoint union of lit cuboids. Each command subtracts its region from existing cuboids by peeling at most six disjoint slabs, then adds the command cuboid only when it is on. Initialization clips every command to the inclusive region [-50,50] on each axis. Positive volume products and totals are checked. The independent oracle uses signed inclusion-exclusion instead.

Day 23 represents a burrow as fixed hallway and room cells. Parsing enforces room depth, species counts, empty doorways and contiguous room occupancy. Moves enter only the deepest available cell of a room containing no other species, and never stop in a doorway. A correctly placed amphipod above only its own species stays put: leaving cannot clear a route needed by another species.

The priority search uses recorded best costs and a lower bound that ignores blockers and deeper destination cells. It retains both homeward and hallway moves instead of depending on forced-home pruning. Under the parser's occupancy and count invariants, zero remaining energy means the complete goal state. Searches bound expanded, recorded and queued states at two million each. Part-two unfolding requires the original two-row, empty-hallway layout.

Day 24 validates every instruction in each eighteen-instruction MONAD block before extracting constants. The supported program has at most eighteen blocks, balanced push/pop structure, divisors one or twenty-six, addends zero through sixteen, push offsets ten through twenty-five and pop offsets minus twenty-five through nine. Other programs return an explicit unsupported-program error.

These bounds make every pushed base-26 digit lie from one through twenty-five. A push always increases stack height; a matched pop decreases it; a mismatched pop preserves it. Balanced programs can therefore end at zero only if every pop matches. For each pair, `popDigit = pushDigit + pushAddend + popOffset`; feasible digits lie from one through nine. Maximizing or minimizing the earlier push digit independently chooses the lexicographically extremal complete model. A checked literal interpreter then executes the original program with those digits, preserving division truncation, modulo preconditions and arithmetic failures.

The original program yields `99196997985942` and `84191521311611`, both ending at zero. The independent oracle records all seven paired constraints and final registers. No program constants or final model numbers are embedded in the solver.

Day 25 applies simultaneous eastward moves, then simultaneous southward moves, with wraparound in each phase. It counts the first stationary step as one. Repeated moving states return an error; storage and work are bounded by one million grid cells, an estimated sixty-four MiB of history and one hundred million cell visits.

## Reference comparisons

Earlier-day comparisons used collected `erikw/advent-of-code-solutions` days 7 and 12, `skazhy/advent`'s Python day 15, and `jpverkamp/advent-of-code` days 13 and 14. Mean candidates, heap Dijkstra and set reflections were retained. Explicit path materialization, rebuilt expanded risk graphs and linked polymer chains were unnecessary; finite visit states, lazy risks and pair counts own those computations instead.

Days 16–22 were compared with the collected `dschlyter/advent-of-code` files `julia/2021/day16.jl` through `day22.jl`. Direct packet evaluation, proper rotations, lookup-driven backgrounds and bounded position/score memoization fit the Go models. Fixed projectile limits, permissive payload overruns, evaluated snailfish input and rebuilding transformed beacon sets for every candidate were rejected. Disjoint cuboid subtraction keeps union ownership explicit without recursive overlapping volume calculations.

For days 23–25, the collected [maneatingape Scala edition](https://github.com/maneatingape/advent-of-code-scala/tree/063a22ee01c1d489f76851e4e4971b0b9b767903/src/main/scala/AdventOfCode2021) confirms the room/hallway model, paired MONAD constraints and two cucumber phases. The Go version uses recorded best-cost search, validates the full ALU template, verifies derived models by literal execution and reports moving cycles. These checks replace assumptions tied to a particular personal input.

The performance review found no material work-accounting issue in days 16–25. These include test-process overhead and are workload observations, not benchmark comparisons or speedup claims. Worst-admitted-input latency and peak memory were not measured; storage-accounting limits are not RSS guarantees.

The full `go test -race -shuffle=on -count=1 -timeout=3m ./...` run passed, including every personal fixture; the 2021 package took 13.111 seconds under race instrumentation. `staticcheck ./...`, `go mod tidy`, formatting and `git diff --check` passed. Coverage was checked against actual Go symbols: 464 implemented parts and 49 remaining in 2022.

Completion review covered all 2021 solvers and tests, shared contracts, fixtures, oracle and documentation, with no material findings.
