## Ownership and source corrections

Each day owns its puzzle behavior. Existing numeric conversion, scanners, grid coordinates, collections, heaps and search helpers are reused. `elfcode.go` owns the sixteen register-machine operations, operand validation, checked arithmetic and separate instruction-pointer semantics shared by days 16, 19 and 21. The shared OCR package owns pixel validation and glyph extraction for fixed-cell six-row displays and separated eight/ten-row displays.

The ports correct source behavior that the original personal-input goldens did not expose:

- Frequency repetition starts with zero and has a finite no-repeat decision. Equal-length IDs are compared without truncation.
- Voronoi ties have no owner; safe regions can extend beyond the sites' rectangle. Scheduling releases every completion at a timestamp before assigning new work. Marble processing includes the last marble.
- Point alignment finds a global area minimum. Power-grid squares include every legal edge and size. Plant extrapolation requires a repeated normalized shape and translation, and handles extinction.
- Carts have stable identities, remove collisions immediately and terminate when none survive. Recipe matching checks every appended digit and preserves leading zeros; part one slices exactly the requested ten scores.
- Grid combat separates target-square and first-step reading-order decisions, preserves unit identity, skips dead units and counts only complete rounds.
- Opcode deduction rejects inconsistent or ambiguous mappings. Candidate-specific invalid operands do not invalidate other possible immediate operations.
- Water starts at the actual spring and distinguishes flowing from settled water. Lumber cycles compare whole grids, with zero-valued resource categories handled normally.
- Route branches retain all endpoints, then shortest paths are computed on the completed door graph.
- VM shortcuts derive their values from supplied programs. Cave geology keeps the actual target independent of exploration bounds; rescue ends with the torch equipped.
- Nanobot coverage is optimized in three dimensions. Army combat handles stalemates, current attack strength and all target-selection priorities; minimum boosts are checked in ascending order.

All original personal answers remain unchanged under these independently verified rules.

## Decisions and proofs

### Finite frequency analysis

Day 1 compares first-cycle prefix frequencies and cycle drift. Compatible prefix pairs determine the earliest repeated frequency without cycling indefinitely or multiplying a potentially enormous repeat time. First-cycle accumulation is checked in `int64`; later-cycle reasoning is mathematical and uses unsigned differences between signed prefixes. The implementation does not promise that every conceptual intermediate frequency of an arbitrarily late repeat fits `int64`.

### Global alignment and shared OCR

Day 10 computes the last positive crossing of every pair of affine coordinates. After its ceiling, coordinate ordering is fixed and both bounding dimensions are nondecreasing, so no later area can improve. The solver checks every integer time from zero through that bound, choosing the earliest tie and comparing areas with exact 128-bit multiplication.

The actual 308-point input has horizon 10097 and global minimum at 10036, requiring 3,110,184 point evaluations. QA independently checked piecewise-quadratic extrema between crossing times. The resulting 62-by-10 raster decodes to `JJXZHKFP`.

`DecodeSeparatedDisplay` supports the official eight-row H/I example and the established ten-row alphabet `ABCEFGHJKLNPRXZ`. The ten-row masks come from [mstksg's OCR font](https://github.com/mstksg/advent-of-code-ocr/blob/main/src/Advent/OCR/LetterMap.hs), exact Git blob `589f672412d7be309e5f4e63a76df96e43de54bc`. An independent advisor matched all fifteen masks and confirmed the dark-column segmentation assumption. Both OCR entry points share validation and mask extraction; existing six-row callers retain their fixed-cell layout contract.

### Register machines

The VM writes its separate instruction pointer into the bound register before an instruction, then reads that register and increments the separate pointer afterward. The official sample consequently returns register zero equal to six, not seven.

Day 19 recognizes the complete divisor-loop body, executes initialization literally, derives the target, and restores the full loop-exit register state after summing divisors. Execution resumes in the supplied tail, which remains authoritative for the final answer. The actual targets are 989 and 10551389, yielding 1056 and 10915260. Arithmetic guards preserve the checked interpreter's behavior; unrecognized programs use bounded literal execution.

One per-run allowance charges every instruction dispatch and every divisor candidate. Accelerator budget exhaustion returns an error before committing the replacement register state. This prevents repeated loop entries from hiding expensive divisor work behind a nominal instruction limit.

Day 21 recognizes the complete thirty-one-instruction recurrence, validates distinct register roles, matching masks and division constants, initialization and arithmetic bounds, and derives all constants from the input. Comparison discovery follows the nonhalting branch even through zero. The original program produces first comparison 13443200, and 13555 unique comparisons ending with 7717135 before repetition. An independent literal VM also reaches the first comparison after 1846 instructions. Unsupported program shapes return an explicit error rather than applying the source's inline seed to unrelated code.

Day 22 grows the erosion cache as needed while keeping zero geological index at the mouth and actual target. Existing A* receives a heuristic of Manhattan distance plus seven when the torch still needs equipping; only the correct target state has heuristic zero. The independent exact search found risk 5622 and rescue time 1089.

The independent optimum is `(12613106,31777547,16083427)`, covered by 974 bots at distance 60474080. Radial interval overlap was rejected because it does not prove a common three-dimensional point.

Grid combat uses two BFS passes for the two reading-order decisions. Elf powers are checked from three through two hundred; at two hundred every goblin dies in one hit, so greater power cannot change the trajectory. Independent canonical battles anchor both simulators.

## Bounds and errors

Limits produce errors, never partial answers. Sky alignment admits at most two thousand points, a two-million-second horizon, one hundred million point evaluations and one million raster pixels. Plant cycles have update, width and stored-shape budgets. Cart simulation has a tick budget. Recipe search stores at most one hundred million scores. Grid combat bounds arena cells and rounds.

Water simulation bounds depth, stored cells and fill operations. Lumber cycle storage has a sixty-four-MiB estimate. Route expansion bounds expression size, room count and set operations. Cave exploration bounds erosion storage and expansions. Nanobot subdivision bounds intersection checks and queued cubes. Army boosts and aggregate round work have explicit limits. Constellation search admits at most five thousand points and reuses BFS for transitive four-dimensional connectivity.

Input errors, undefined transitions, unsupported glyphs, unreachable or ambiguous results, arithmetic overflow and budget exhaustion remain distinct from valid computed results. Shared tests cover read failures at every day entry point.
